I study how intelligent systems process spoken language, anticipate conversational turns, and interact naturally with humans under real-time computational constraints.

Through p99lab, I develop and openly release speech and conversational AI models, emphasizing rigorous evaluation, computational efficiency, and accessibility.

I completed my master’s at the University of California, Davis. My work connects practical speech AI with a broader interest in the computational principles of human communication and cognition.

Causal spoken dialogue2026

turn-1-miniEfficient causal end-of-turn prediction

A 5.3M-parameter, audio-only model for predicting when a speaker has finished. The original v1 release studies strictly causal turn-taking with streaming inference on a single CPU core.

Conversational modeling2026

turn-1Conversational turn detection

A separate, larger model for distinguishing completed turns from pauses and hesitation. It studies acoustic evidence together with optional context from the agent’s previous utterance.

A note on research

I am interested in intelligence beyond predictive accuracy: how humans process language incrementally, anticipate interactions, and communicate under uncertainty.

I believe research should be accessible, reproducible, and open to scrutiny. Open models and documented evaluations make it possible for others to examine, challenge, and build on the work.
